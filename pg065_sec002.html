Activity 7.2: Reading for specific information

(a) Read the following conversations between the teacher and the pupils in the classroom.

Teacher:
Good morning, class!
Class:
Good morning, Teacher!
Teacher:
Today, we are going to talk about another important way of reading. Yesterday, we learnt about reading for general information. Can anyone remind me what that means?
Pupil 1:
It’s when you read to understand the main idea, but not all the details.
Teacher:
That’s correct! Well done! Today, we will talk about a different kind of reading called “reading for specific information.” Does anyone know what that might mean?
Pupil 2:
Maybe it means reading to find something special?
Teacher:
Very close! When we read for specific information, we are looking for something very particular or a small detail in the text. We focus on finding exact facts or answers, not just the general idea.
Pupil 3:
Like if we want to know how many elephants live in a park?
Teacher:
Exactly! If you’re reading a book or a paragraph and you need to know how many elephants live in a park, you’re looking for specific information.
