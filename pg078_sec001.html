Pendo listened carefully as Mr Mussa explained the first type of wish: Birthday Wishes. “When someone is celebrating their birthday,” Mr Mussa said, “it’s a special day for them. We can wish them a ‘Happy Birthday’ to make them feel joyful and loved.” He wrote an example on the board: “Happy Birthday! May your day be filled with fun, laughter, and lots of cake!” Then, he added, “And to make it even more special, you can mention something they love. For example: ‘Happy Birthday! I hope you get to play your favourite game today!’”

Pendo smiled, imagining writing a birthday wish for her best friend, Samwel, who loved playing basketball.

Next, Mr Mussa talked about Best Wishes for Exams. “When our friends or classmates have exams, we can send them good wishes to encourage them to do their best,” he explained. He shared a simple example: “Good luck on your exam! I know you have worked hard, and you will do great!” Mr Mussa continued, “You could also say, ‘Do your best and believe in yourself. You are going to succeed!’”

Pendo thought about her friend Maria, who often felt nervous before exams. She decided to write a supportive wish for her to help boost her confidence.

Finally, Mr Mussa discussed Get Well Soon Wishes. “If someone we know is not feeling well, we can send them a wish to help them feel better,” he said. He wrote on the board: “Get well soon! We miss you and hope you feel better quickly.” Then, he added, “You could also add something comforting, like, ‘Sending you lots of love and hugs. I hope you feel stronger and healthier every day!’”

Pendo nodded, thinking of her cousin who had been feeling sick. She knew just what to write to cheer her up.
