Mwanaidi, a friendly fruit seller, carefully arranged her guava and watermelon on her stall. Each fruit had a price tag attached, with the guavas going for 100 shillings each, and the watermelons for 2000 shillings.

Next to her, Rejo, another seller, was busy displaying his tomatoes and onions in neat piles. He smiled as he caught sight of Mwanaidi.

“Good morning, Mwanaidi! How is business!” Rejo asked, wiping sweat from his brow.

“Morning, Rejo! It’s going well, but I wish more buyers would come early.” Mwanaidi replied.

Just then, a mother and her little boy approached Mwanaidi’s stall. The boy’s eyes lit up as he pointed at a ripe watermelon. “Mum, can we buy one of those big watermelons?” He asked eagerly.

The mother smiled. “How much does it cost?” She asked, turning to Mwanaidi. “It’s 2000 shillings, but since you are my first buyer today, I will give you a discount. You can have it for 1500 shillings.” Mwanaidi said, beaming.

“Thank you! We will take it.” The mother replied, handing over the money.

As the woman left with her purchase, Mwanaidi turned to Rejo. “See, the key to selling is knowing when to offer a little discount!”

Meanwhile, on the other side of the market, Yobu was selling colourful clothes and utensils. He watched as a woman examined one of the vibrant fabrics and utensils.

“How much is this rice cooker?” She asked.

“That one is 40,000 shillings,” Yobu said, pointing to the price tag.
